# Lesson 1.2: Ear Training (Theme A: Social Ping-Pong)

**Objective:** Decode spoken greetings, conversational cadence, connected speech, and reductions at native speed with zero written Kurdish crutches.
**Duration:** ~2.5 minutes (8 screens)

---

## Screen 1: Intonation Radar (Question vs. Statement)
**Type:** Auditory Discrimination

**UI Elements:**
- Two visual icons: [ ❓ Question (Rising Pitch ↗) ] and [ 💬 Statement (Falling Pitch ↘) ]

**Audio & Mechanics:** Audio plays native cadence: "How are you? ↗". Learner has 4 seconds to tap the Question icon. Audio then plays "I'm good, thank you ↘" → learner taps Statement. Trains the ear to track pitch contours rather than reading punctuation.

---

## Screen 2: The Greeting Catcher (Fast Acoustic Contrast)
**Type:** Minimal Distinction

**UI Elements:**
- 3 clean English buttons: [ Hey ], [ Hi ], [ Hello ]. No translations.

**Audio & Mechanics:** Fast conversational audio plays: "Hey Ahmad!" The learner must tap [ Hey ] within 3 seconds. Next clip plays: "Hi Sara!" → tap [ Hi ]. Punishes hesitation and locks auditory recognition of single-syllable greetings.

---

## Screen 3: Mood Decoder (Acoustic Adjective Identification)
**Type:** Semantic Listening

**UI Elements:**
- 4 mood cards displaying emoji + English labels:
  - fine (neutral smile)
  - good (thumbs up)
  - great (big grin / stars)
  - cool (sunglasses)

**Audio & Mechanics:** Audio plays: "I'm great, thank you!" with heavy vocal emphasis on great. Learner taps the great card. Next audio plays flat, relaxed: "I'm cool, thank you." → learner selects cool.

---

## Screen 4: The Connected Speed Trap ("How are you?")
**Type:** Connected Speech Perception

**UI Elements:**
- Two speed buttons: [ 🐢 Slow / Articulated ] and [ ⚡ Natural Native Speed ]

**Audio & Mechanics:** Learner taps slow button → hears robotic citation form: "How — are — you?" Learner taps speed button → hears natural reduction and vowel linking: /haʊərjuː/ (هەواریو). System prompts: "Which one sounds like a real conversation?" Learner taps the Natural tile to reinforce native listening expectations.

---

## Screen 5: The Reduction Decoder ("What about you?")
**Type:** Slang / American Flap-T Training

**UI Elements:**
- Waveform audio player with prompt: "What did you hear?"
- 3 text choices:
  - [ What about you? ]
  - [ How are you? ]
  - [ Thank you ]

**Audio & Mechanics:** Audio plays rapid American casual reduction (/wʌt əbaʊt juː/ → وەرەباوتیو؟). Learner must select [ What about you? ] without slow enunciation help.

---

## Screen 6: Blind Audio Scramble (Dialogue Sequencing)
**Type:** Conversational Logic by Ear

**UI Elements:**
- 3 unlabeled audio play buttons: [ 🔈 Audio A ], [ 🔈 Audio B ], [ 🔈 Audio C ]
- 3 empty sequential slots: 1st [ ___ ] → 2nd [ ___ ] → 3rd [ ___ ]

**Audio & Mechanics:**
- Audio A: "I'm good, thank you. What about you?"
- Audio B: "Hey Ahmad, how are you?"
- Audio C: "I'm cool, thank you."

User taps to listen, then drags audio bubbles into proper chronological order (B → A → C). Zero text crutches.

---

## Screen 7: Social Context Detection
**Type:** Receptive Context Check

**UI Elements:**
- Two illustrated scenario cards:
  - Scenario 1: A student waving to a teacher at a desk
  - Scenario 2: Two friends greeting each other on the street

**Audio & Mechanics:** Audio plays casual street tone: "Hey! How are you?". Learner taps Scenario 2 (Friends). Audio then plays: "Hello, teacher." → Learner taps Scenario 1. Builds ear for register and formality.

---

## Screen 8: The Speed Ear Gate (Lesson Checkpoint)
**Type:** Rapid Fire Evaluation

**UI Elements:**
- Center countdown ring (3 seconds per rep)
- 2 response options: [ True ] or [ False ]

**Audio & Mechanics:**
- Rep 1: Audio says "I'm cool, thank you." On-screen: sunglasses emoji. → Tap True
- Rep 2: Audio says "Hi Ahmad." On-screen: Sara's photo. → Tap False
- Rep 3: Audio says "What about you?" On-screen text: "How are you?" → Tap False

Scoring ≥2/3 unlocks Lesson 1.3 (Speaking Training).
